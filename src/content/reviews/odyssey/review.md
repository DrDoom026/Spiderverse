## The Odyssey

Hey, so I watched The Odyssey by Christopher Nolan in IMAX, and God, that's a once-in-a-lifetime experience. I had a lot to talk about, so it's going to be long.

Let's start with the opening. The movie opens with the Trojan Horse washed up on the shore of a beach, and that shot looked amazing. This movie is visually pleasing. Frames of the boat marching toward the endless horizon are breathtaking, and you can just appreciate the cinematography throughout the movie. Every island has its own visual aesthetic. Hades is covered in black sand, and the low-light shots are so beautiful that you can't stop appreciating every frame. When they enter the city of Troy at night, that shot is amazing. You can feel the flares flickering across the screen, and it all feels alive. You'll genuinely feel like you're in that sequence.

To be honest, I hadn't watched a movie this picturesque since Dune: Part Two. You can tell why Christopher Nolan is considered one of the best directors of this generation. He is more famous than the cast itself.

Now let's talk about the background score. The score is peak, as always. Ludwig Goransson did a great job. The instruments used to create this unique score were specially developed for this movie. They recreated instruments from ancient Ithaca through extensive research and made them as authentic as possible, and you can really feel the work they put into it. The score feels alive, and it's one of the major aspects of the movie. And about Polyphemus' cry—it blew my ears out, but it was on purpose, so we could feel the suffering and pain the soldiers were experiencing.

This movie is literally made for IMAX. They shot the whole movie in IMAX, and you can absolutely witness the greatness, artistic visuals, and breathtaking frames on the bigger screen. It is an experience.

Some details I noticed in the movie are just peak. Like, the biggest strength of Odysseus is not his physical power but his wit. The bow, Palintonos, that only he can string can actually only be strung backwards, so no one except him can string that bow. Likewise, the movie is philosophy-heavy, and you can find meaningful details in every moment. During the Sirens' song, Nolan didn't let us hear the song ourselves, but that's the whole point. We experience Odysseus hearing the song from the perspective of the soldiers. And the biggest monster that stole seven years from him was actually the comfort of Calypso.

Talking about the CGI and VFX, we know that Nolan hates CGI and VFX the most, and that's one of the main reasons he never fails to deliver. He created real sets, shot in the real ocean with a Viking ship, and the Sirens are actually real people sitting on cliffs in the middle of the ocean. The effort really shines throughout the movie. The Cyclops, the giants, and one of the most amazing scenes—the witch Circe turning men into pigs—it's all done practically, and that's just mind-blowing.

Talking about the performances, I mean, they're absolutely incredible. You can feel the emotional heaviness of Penelope, the guilt of Odysseus, the lost path of Telemachus, the pitiful nature of Antinous, and the aura of Agamemnon. Even though he gets very little screen time, he is just aura farming throughout the whole movie. I just loved the characters and their performances.

One of the best parts of this movie is its dialogue. I mean, Odysseus explaining the Sirens' song is printed in my mind. The monologue starts with:

"All the things you wanted it to, then all the things you wished you never wished for... It told you what you most want is what you most can't have, and that the thing you most can't have is what you already had and lost."

And damn, that's just something really deep.

The ending bow scene, where everyone fails to string the bow, and at last Odysseus picks it up, strings it, and when he pulls the string... that scene is just going to be one of the best moments I have ever experienced in cinema. The moment he pulls the string and that heavy note echoes throughout the hall, Penelope turns sideways and instantly knows that her husband is back. That moment is just... I can't even describe it in words. The deep background score begins and ends with the pull of the string. That's just amazing.

Talking about the ending, the biggest irony is that Odysseus is known for his honesty and his wit, yet it was a trick, a deception, a chicanery that he used to enter the city of Troy that made him famous and became the song that was sung. You can feel the guilt he carries, the guilt that haunts him because of what he did. He isn't proud of it. The man who left his home wasn't the same man who returned.

"A face, a war, a thought, a trick... a trick to break the walls of Troy and burn it screaming to the ground!"

This movie is just peak cinema.
